# 2. System Overview

## 2.1 System Description

The dsf (Wideband RF Receiver) is a high-performance, multi-octave radio frequency (RF) to digital conversion system designed to operate in harsh military environments. The system functions as a superheterodyne receiver that captures RF signals between 5.0 GHz and 18.0 GHz, processes them through a series of amplification, filtering, and frequency conversion stages, and ultimately digitizes the signal using a 10 GSPS Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC). The digitized data is transported via a high-speed LVDS interface compliant with the JESD204B standard.

The receiver is architected to support a 3 GHz instantaneous bandwidth (IBW), enabling the capture of wideband waveforms such as frequency-hopping signals, chirp radar pulses, and high-data-rate communications. A critical design goal is the maintenance of signal integrity across the entire frequency band, characterized by a stringent Noise Figure (NF) of 6–8 dB and a Spurious-Free Dynamic Range (SFDR) of 81–90 dB.

### 2.1.1 Operational Flow
The operational signal flow begins at the 50-Ohm RF interface. Signals first encounter a protective Limiter stage designed to clamp transient high-power events (e.g., up to +5 dBm continuous or higher pulsed power) to protect sensitive downstream components. Following protection, the signal enters a Low Noise Amplifier (LNA) stage utilizing the Analog Devices HMC698LP4E. This stage sets the system noise floor while providing approximately 22 dB of gain. After amplification, the signal passes through a Bandpass Filter (BPF) to remove out-of-band noise and spurious content before reaching the downconversion mixer.

Frequency translation is performed by the HMC1048LC4 Double-Balanced Mixer. This component mixes the incoming RF signal with a Local Oscillator (LO) signal generated by the Texas Instruments LMX2594 synthesizer. The LO frequency is variable to allow the selection of specific 3 GHz slices of the 5–18 GHz input spectrum for downconversion to an Intermediate Frequency (IF) range suitable for the ADC.

The downconverted IF signal is conditioned by a Variable Gain Amplifier (VGA), specifically the HMC698LP2, which provides up to 42 dB of gain adjustment in 1 dB steps via a Serial Peripheral Interface (SPI). This Automatic Gain Control (AGC) loop ensures that the signal presented to the ADC utilizes the full dynamic range of the converter without clipping. The final stage involves digitization by the TI ADC10DX300, which samples the conditioned analog signal at 10 GSPS. The digital data is then serialized and output over JESD204B lanes to FPGA or DSP back-end processing.

### 2.1.2 Control and Management
The dsf system features a dedicated Control Interface (SPI/I2C) responsible for configuring the LO frequency, setting the VGA gain, and managing the ADC lane initialization. The system is designed to operate autonomously once configured, maintaining lock and performance characteristics across the full military temperature range of -55°C to +125°C.

## 2.3 System Architecture

### 2.3.1 Signal Chain Architecture
The signal chain adopts a heterodyne architecture optimized for wideband instantaneous bandwidth (3 GHz) rather than a narrowband channelized approach. This architectural choice necessitates a high-speed RF sampling ADC capable of directly digitizing the IF stage output or a low IF.

The selection of the 3 GHz bandwidth is driven by the ADC10DX300’s analog input bandwidth (-3 dB point), allowing for direct sampling of IF signals up to the 3rd Nyquist zone. The LMX2594 LO synthesizer provides the necessary frequency agility; it generates a low-phase-noise clock reference that mixes with the incoming RF to translate the desired frequency band to the ADC’s optimal capture window.

#### Gain Distribution
To achieve the required system linearity (+5 dBm P1dB) and Noise Figure (6-8 dB), the gain is distributed across multiple stages:
1.  **Front-End Gain (+22 dB):** Provided by the HMC698LP4E LNA. This high gain at the front end suppresses the noise contribution of subsequent stages (mixer, filters).
2.  **Driver/IF Gain (+18 dB):** Provided by the ADA4817-1. This stage amplifies the converted IF signal to a level sufficient to drive the ADC's input capacitance effectively.
3.  **Variable Gain (0 to +42 dB):** Provided by the HMC698LP2. This stage is digitally controlled to scale the signal amplitude to match the ADC’s full-scale range (typically 1 Vpp or 0 dBm), preventing saturation from strong signals and optimizing the SNR for weak signals.

### 2.3.2 Digital Architecture
The digital subsystem centers on the TI ADC10DX300. This component integrates the analog-to-digital conversion functions with a JESD204B physical layer (PHY). The ADC utilizes a dual-lane or quad-lane JESD204B interface (configuration dependent on lane rate) to serialize the 10-bit samples.
*   **Lane Rate:** At 10 GSPS and utilizing 8b/10b encoding and frame aggregation, the raw data rate is approximately 100 Gbps. Assuming a configuration of 8 lanes at ~12.5 Gbps/lane or 16 lanes at lower speeds to fit standard FPGA SerDes capabilities.
*   **Latency:** The JESD204B interface introduces deterministic latency, which is critical for time-domain analysis of the received signals.

### 2.3.3 Frequency Planning (Derived)
While the specific IF frequency is configurable via the LO, the architecture supports the following general frequency translation:
$$f_{IF} = |f_{RF} - f_{LO}|$$
The system is designed such that the LO is swept to step through the 5–18 GHz RF band in 3 GHz chunks, downconverting them to a fixed IF band (e.g., 1.5–4.5 GHz) that sits within the 3 GHz analog bandwidth of the ADC.

### 2.3.4 Power Architecture
The power supply network is designed to isolate sensitive analog circuitry from the noisy digital switching of the ADC and FPGA interface.
*   **+12V Rail:** Supplies the high-power RF components (LNA, Mixer, LO). This voltage allows for high linearity (P1dB) and output power capabilities.
*   **+5V / +3.3V Rails:** Supplies the IF amplifiers and VGA.
*   **+1.2V Rail:** The core supply for the ADC digital logic. Strict ripple rejection (<1% noise) is required on this rail to maintain ADC Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) performance.
*   **Sequencing:** Power-up sequencing is managed to ensure the RF path is powered and stable before the digital FPGA interface attempts JESD204B link initialization.

*   **DC Offset Calibration:** The ADC requires periodic calibration to correct for DC offsets introduced by the mixer and amplifiers.

The high frequencies (up to 18 GHz RF, 10 Gbps LVDS) impose severe restrictions on package parasitics.

    *   **Constraint**: PCB footprint must accommodate 0.8mm pitch balls. Escape routing requires microvias (laser drilled) to maintain signal integrity for the JESD204B lanes.
*   **Decoupling**:
    *   All high-speed devices (ADC, FPGA) require 0201 or 01005 size decoupling capacitors placed as close as possible to supply pins to minimize loop inductance.

1.  **Power Dissipation**:
    *   The total power budget is fixed at **26W - 50W** (REQ-HW-008).

## 4.3 Manufacturing Constraints

This section defines the limitations and requirements for the fabrication and assembly of the dsf hardware.

### 4.3.1 PCB Fabrication (Layer Stackup and Materials)
The 10 GSPS ADC and 18 GHz RF front-end demand advanced PCB materials.

1.  **Laminate Material**:
    *   Standard FR-4 is strictly **prohibited** for RF and high-speed digital layers due to dielectric loss ($D_f$).
    *   **Material Selection**: Hydrocarbon Ceramic laminates (e.g., Rogers RO3003 or RO4350B) are mandatory for RF layers.
    *   **Stackup Constraint**: A hybrid stackup is required. The RF layers shall use Rogers material; the digital power/ground planes may use high-performance FR-4 (e.g., Isola 370HR) to manage cost, provided transition regions are bonded correctly.

2.  **Dielectric Thickness**:
    *   **Impedance Control**: The RF input is 50 $\Omega$. For microstrip/stripline on Rogers material ($\epsilon_r \approx 3.5$), dielectric thickness shall be 8 mils to 12 mils to achieve trace widths of 15-20 mils, which are manufacturable and low-loss.

3.  **Drill Aspect Ratio**:
    *   For 10 GSPS LVDS escape routing from the ADC BGA, laser-drilled microvias (4 mil / 100 $\mu m$ diameter) are mandatory for layer transitions.
    *   Aspect ratio shall not exceed 10:1 for plated through holes (PTH) to ensure reliable plating.

### 4.3.2 Assembly and Soldering
*   **Lead-Free Solder**: SAC305 (Sn96.5/Ag3.0/Cu0.5) shall be the default solder alloy.
*   **Reflow Profile**: Due to the presence of the QFN LNA and BGA ADC, the reflow profile must comply with the component with the lowest thermal tolerance (likely the plastic packages).
*   **Underfill**: The ADC10DX300 BGA and the HMC698LP4E QFNs must be staked or underfilled to mitigate CTE mismatch stresses during thermal cycling between -55°C and +125°C. This prevents solder joint cracking over the product lifecycle.

### 4.3.3 Tuning and Calibration
Manufacturing yield for microwave circuits often requires post-assembly tuning.

*   **RF Tuning**: The design shall incorporate provisions for tuning the RF matching networks. This includes:
    *   **"Option" Pads**: Locations on the PCB where capacitors or inductors can be added/removed to tune the center frequency (11.5 GHz) and bandwidth (3 GHz).
    *   **Laser Trimming**: Not used for RF, but potentiometers for DC bias calibration may be required for the VGA offsets.
*   **ADC Calibration**: The firmware must perform a background gain/offset correction cycle at startup to account for inter-symbol interference (ISI) on the JESD204B lanes.

### 4.3.4 Inspection Requirements
*   **X-Ray Inspection**: 100% X-Ray inspection is mandatory for the ADC BGA and any hidden solder joints of the Mixer and LNA to verify voiding (voids < 15% per IPC-A-610 Class 3) and alignment.
*   **AOI**: Automated Optical Inspection is required for all chip components (0402/0201 sizes) to detect tombstoning or misalignment.

### 5.1.1 RF Functional and Performance Testing

#### Test Case 1: Input Matching and Frequency Range
*   **Requirement ID:** REQ-HW-001, REQ-HW-013
*   **Test Method:** T
*   **Description:** Measure Input VSWR and Return Loss across the 5-18 GHz band.

    1.  Calibrate PNA-X at the end of the test cable.
    2.  Measure S11 (Reflection Coefficient).
    3.  Sweep frequency from 5 GHz to 18 GHz.
    4.  Record VSWR and Return Loss.
*   **Pass Criteria:** VSWR < 2.0:1 (Return Loss > 9.54 dB) across 5–18 GHz.

#### Test Case 2: Gain and Linearity (P1dB)
*   **Requirement ID:** REQ-HW-005, REQ-HW-012
*   **Test Method:** T
*   **Description:** Verify system gain linearity and Input P1dB.
*   **Equipment:** Signal Generator (up to 20 GHz), Spectrum Analyzer.
*   **Setup:** Signal Generator -> RF Input. Output monitored via JESD204B data capture interface or coupled IF port.
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Set LO to 11.5 GHz.
    2.  Input RF tone at 12 GHz (resulting in 500 MHz IF).
    3.  Increase input power from -60 dBm to +10 dBm in 1 dB steps.
    4.  Record output power vs. input power.
    5.  Identify point where gain drops by 1 dB from linear region.
*   **Pass Criteria:**
    *   Input P1dB $\ge$ +5 dBm.
    *   Output Power at P1dB $\ge$ +5 dBm (assuming nominal gain).

*   **Requirement ID:** REQ-HW-002
*   **Test Method:** T
*   **Description:** Verify amplitude response across a 3 GHz instantaneous bandwidth.
*   **Equipment:** Signal Generator, Spectrum Analyzer.
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Set LO to center frequency (e.g., 10 GHz).
    2.  Sweep input RF frequency from LO + 100 MHz to LO + 3.1 GHz.
    3.  Record normalized gain.
*   **Pass Criteria:** Gain flatness within ±1.5 dB across any 3 GHz window.

#### Test Case 4: Noise Figure (NF) and Sensitivity
*   **Requirement ID:** REQ-HW-003
*   **Test Method:** T
*   **Description:** Measure system Noise Figure using the Y-Factor method.
*   **Equipment:** Noise Source (346C/k), NFA (Noise Figure Analyzer).
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Connect Noise Source to RF Input.
    2.  Set system to Maximum Gain.
