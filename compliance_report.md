## Risk Items Requiring Human Review

1. **Component Traceability for Military Applications**: While all components meet the basic temperature requirements for military applications, full MIL-STD compliance requires additional documentation including traceability, lot tracking, and possibly qualifying additional specifications.

2. **FCC Certification for Complete System**: Although individual components meet relevant standards, the complete system will require FCC certification testing to verify emissions compliance, particularly for the high-speed digital interfaces and RF front-end.

3. **CE Marking Documentation**: While components are compliant with relevant directives, the complete system will require technical documentation and a Declaration of Conformity for CE marking.

4. **EMI Filter Effectiveness**: The EMI filter selection is appropriate based on specifications, but actual performance should be verified during system testing to ensure MIL-STD-461 compliance is achieved.

## Recommendations

1. **Component Documentation**: Obtain and maintain RoHS, REACH, and other relevant compliance documentation for all components to support regulatory submissions.

2. **System-Level Testing**: Plan for system-level EMC testing to verify FCC Part 15 and MIL-STD-461 compliance as these cannot be assessed at component level alone.

3. **Design Considerations**: 
   - Implement proper shielding for high-speed digital components
   - Ensure adequate grounding for RF components to minimize emissions
   - Include EMI filtering on all external interfaces

4. **Documentation**: Prepare technical files for CE marking including risk assessments, test reports, and design verification.

5. **Alternative Considerations**: While all selected components are compliant, consider having qualified second sources for critical components to mitigate supply chain risks.
